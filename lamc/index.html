Institute of Plant Molecular Biology · Biology Centre CAS

Sequence composition, molecular organization and evolution of plant genomes and chromosomes

The laboratory examines sequence composition, molecular organization and evolution of plant genomes and chromosomes. Our research is currently centered around the following topics:

{% if site.home_photo_grid %}{% include photo-grid.html %}{% endif %}
01

Repetitive DNA sequences and their impact on genome organization and evolution

We analyze sequence composition and abundance in plant genomes of both major classes of repetitive sequences: transposable elements and satellite repeats. In addition to using experimental methods for repetitive DNA isolation and characterization, we have recently adapted next-generation sequencing followed by bioinformatics analysis of sequencing data for global analysis of repeat composition in complex eukaryotic genomes. We are also interested in molecular mechanisms leading to amplification, sequence homogenization and elimination of repetitive elements in plant genomes.

02

Development of novel bioinformatics approaches for analyzing next-generation sequencing data

We develop software tools for de novo identification and characterization of repetitive elements from genomic shotgun sequencing data obtained by next-generation technologies. In addition to comprehensive characterization of the repeats within a single species we also focus on comparative analysis of global repeat composition between multiple genomes and on analyzing specific types of repeats like satellite DNA.

03

Structure and evolution of plant centromeres

Sequence composition of centromeric regions of plant chromosomes is investigated using a variety of experimental and bioinformatics approaches. We focus on selected groups of closely related species that developed contrasting patterns of centromere organization, including monocentric, meta-polycentric and holocentric chromosomes.
